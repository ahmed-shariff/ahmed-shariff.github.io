abstract: "Thumb-to-finger (T2F) word gesture keyboards, where the user swipes their thumb across the index, middle, and ring fingers to type, offer a promising approach for eyes-free text entry. Prior work has not characterized how T2F gesture input degrades without visual guidance. We investigate the performance characteristics of eyes-free T2F text entry through an initial data collection study with N=12 participants. Offline analysis, using a lightweight decoder, measures the true eyes-free penalty without obfuscation. Results show that combining a language-model error correction and per-user layout calibration recovers much of the text entry accuracy lost in the eyes-free condition. A subsequent evaluation with N=16 participants confirms these findings, with users retaining 61.8% of their eyes-on text entry speed when operating eyes-free after minimal onboarding. Our evaluation establishes a conservative lower bound for eyes-free T2F text entry performance, demonstrating that proprioception-driven T2F interactions are viable for eyes-free text-entry when augmented with context and calibration."
